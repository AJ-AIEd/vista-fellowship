The Pathfinder field guide

Play, tinker,
notice what changes.

A small experiment can make AI’s possibilities and limits easier to judge. Begin with a question from your teaching, keep the conditions visible, and bring your subject knowledge.

Try the supported workflowCompare two systems

Same prompt. Different systems.

  1. Predict before opening a tool.

    Choose a short task in a subject you know well. What do you expect AI to do well, miss, or sound confident about? Keep student-identifying information out of the test.

  2. Hold the conditions steady.

    Paste the same task, criteria, source material, and prompt into two tools you are permitted to use. Record the system, visible model/version, date, and any school or activity instructions. Do not improve the prompt between the first two trials.

    Complete this task as well as you can, following its criteria. State assumptions or missing information.
    Task: [paste the task]
    Criteria: [paste the criteria]

  3. Notice before judging.

    Compare a consequential claim or reasoning step. What differs in accuracy, omissions, framing, voice, cultural assumptions, or confidence? Check against the original source or a trusted disciplinary reference.

  4. Make a classroom connection.

    What could a student trust too quickly? What did you notice because you know the subject? What independent student thinking would remain invisible? Record what the test cannot establish.

Keep a comparison record

Four tools, different roles.

These are possible uses to investigate, not endorsements or promises about learning. Features and access vary. Use your institution’s permitted tools and check their current data policies.

NotebookLM

Try: compare two selected sources on your question. Open a citation and read the passage in context. Then take the controls and check a second claim yourself.

Notice: whether the response represents the source fairly, what it omits, and whether the evidence fits your classroom. A citation is a place to check, not a guarantee.

Flint / Sparky

Try: bring a real teaching dilemma to the activity your facilitator or school has provided. Ask for questions that surface assumptions before asking for suggestions.

Notice: whether the conversation opens your thinking or steers you toward the assistant’s preferred answer. Keep the decision yours. Activity instructions may influence its response.

Open Flint ↗ · Official overview ↗

Use your own school’s activity link when available; VISTA does not provide access to a private Sparky activity.

ChatGPT

Try: run the shared task prompt, then challenge one answer using your own criteria. Ask for an alternative interpretation and verify it independently.

Notice: what changed after you challenged the response, what was still wrong or uncertain, and which judgments required your expertise.

Open ChatGPT ↗

Claude

Try: use the same initial prompt and materials as your other test. Then explore a contrasting interpretation or a possible revision of your task.

Notice: differences in assumptions and framing as well as correctness. Decide what you would accept, change, reject, or investigate further.

Learn with someone.

Watch a colleague check one claim. Then use the tool yourself with a different claim. Explain what you trusted, questioned, rejected, or changed. Record which part you did independently and where support helped.

A route without an AI account.

Read a permitted short extract with a colleague. Compare interpretations and identify what supports each. If using a prepared AI response, label who produced it, the prompt, the system, and the date; do not present a fictional response as an actual test.

Learning is the purpose. You can record a well-reasoned choice to delay, constrain, or exclude AI.
