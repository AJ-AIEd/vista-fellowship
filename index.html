Visibility Into Student Thinking and Agency

If AI can produce the answer, how do we assess the reasoning behind it?

VISTA is a self-guided professional-learning cycle. A teacher takes one assessment they already use, predicts what AI can do with it, tests that prediction, decides what must remain the student’s own thinking, teaches the revised task, and examines the evidence.

A polished product is not evidence of understanding

Many assessments were designed on the assumption that producing the work required doing the thinking. Generative AI weakens that assumption for some tasks and leaves it intact for others. Telling the two apart takes subject knowledge and a look at what the tools actually do with a specific task.

VISTA gives teachers a structured way to do that looking. It does not tell them what to conclude.

What it is not

  • Not an AI detector, and not a way to monitor students.
  • Not an evaluation of teachers. Nothing is scored.
  • Not a lesson or task generator. The guidance on screen is fixed text chosen by the teacher’s own selections; no AI reads or rewrites their responses.
  • Not an argument for more AI. Excluding AI from a task is as defensible an outcome as integrating it.

One assessment, six stages

The first three stages take roughly 60 to 90 minutes before teaching. The last two take 30 to 45 minutes afterwards. These are estimates, not measurements.

  1. Initial AuditWorking alone, the teacher describes the task, names the kinds of learning it requires, predicts what AI could and could not do, and chooses an AI-use level.
  2. AI–Human Thinking Routine (AI-HTR)The teacher tests the prediction with an AI tool of their choice and judges the output by the standards of the discipline and the realities of their classroom.
  3. RedesignThey decide what stays the student’s responsibility, what AI may do, which supports are needed, and what evidence would show understanding.
  4. ImplementationThey teach the task and collect a small amount of evidence that answers their question, without identifying students.
  5. Final AuditThey compare what they expected with what happened, including what the evidence cannot show.
  6. Reflection and Next CycleThey name what changed in their thinking, what is still uncertain, and what they will carry to another assessment.

What a teacher leaves with

A written record of their own reasoning about one assessment: the prediction, the test, the decision, the classroom evidence, and the open questions. It can be printed or saved as a PDF at any stage and kept as a backup file.

The record is theirs. It is stored in their browser, in English or Spanish, and there is no account that anyone else could look into.

What is known so far

VISTA is in early use. It is being piloted during 2026–2027 with a small group of teachers at one international school, facilitated by its author. That pilot is informing the design. It has not yet produced outcome data.

There is no evidence yet that VISTA improves student learning, and the self-guided version has not been studied with teachers working without a facilitator. We say so plainly on the schools page.

Teachers should see what AI does with their own task before deciding what to do about it.
