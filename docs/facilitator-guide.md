# VISTA facilitator guide (draft 0.1)

Status: draft written alongside the self-guided tool. It has not been used with a school and needs the author's review before it is given to anyone.

For the person convening a group of teachers through one VISTA cycle. You do not need to be an AI specialist. Your job is to protect time, keep the conversation on evidence, and keep teachers' notes private.

## Agreements to make first

- Working notes in the tool belong to the teacher. Nobody is asked to show their screen or hand in a report.
- Sharing is by choice. A teacher decides what, if anything, to bring to the group.
- No student names, in the tool, in AI tools, or in discussion.
- The aim is better judgment about one task. Nobody is being assessed, and deciding not to use AI is a legitimate result.

## A cycle in four meetings

**1. Start (45 minutes).** Everyone opens the worked example and reads the Initial Audit and the routine. Discuss: where was the example teacher's prediction wrong, and why did that matter? Then each teacher chooses one real assessment. If some have little experience with AI tools, give twenty minutes to try a task from their own course first.

*Between meetings:* Initial Audit and routine, alone (about an hour).

**2. Redesign clinic (60 minutes).** In pairs across subjects: one describes what the AI trial showed and their draft decision; the other asks only clarifying questions for five minutes, then asks "What evidence would tell you this worked, and what could it miss?" Swap. Finish the Redesign stage individually.

*Between meetings:* teach the task; fill in Implementation while it is fresh.

**3. Evidence conversation (60 minutes).** Each teacher brings one anonymised observation. The group separates what was seen from what it might mean, and offers one alternative explanation. Complete the Final Audit afterwards.

**4. Close (30–45 minutes).** Each teacher states one thing that changed in their thinking, one thing still uncertain, and the next assessment they will look at. Complete Reflection.

## Things that go wrong

- *The audit becomes a hunt for cheating.* Return to the question of what the task is evidence of.
- *Everyone adds a reflection log.* Ask whether the log could be generated as easily as the product.
- *A teacher concludes nothing needs to change.* That is acceptable if they can say what they checked.
- *Discussion drifts to tools.* Park it. The cycle is about one task.

## What not to collect

Do not gather teachers' reports to show activity. If the school wants to learn from the cycle, ask the group what they are willing to share, and remember that in a small group even a summary can point to a person.
