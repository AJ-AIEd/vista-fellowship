Bring One Task

Have the task instructions, learning goal, and success criteria ready. Choose a task you are unsure about or want to improve.

  1. Audit independently. Describe the learning, estimate what AI could do, and choose a SAIL level.
  2. Test and revise. Try the task with AI, check the response, and decide what students must do themselves.
  3. Teach and observe. Explain the AI-use boundaries and collect a few examples that show student understanding.
  4. Reflect. Complete the Final Audit using those examples. Decide what to keep or change.

Brief notes are enough. The tool saves one draft in the current browser on your device. Copy your notes elsewhere before starting another task.

Collect useful evidence

Use Only the Evidence You Need

Choose evidence that answers your question about learning. Use an explanation, decision, or performance you can check. When your existing assessment already provides that evidence, keep it.

Match the uncertaintyUse an AI decision log to trace choices, a brief oral defense to test live understanding, or a revision note to show change over time.
Use none when none is neededWhen you can already observe students demonstrating the learning goal, use that evidence.
Students have a right to say no to AI. Every AI-enabled task needs a meaningful non-AI pathway. Evidence can be verbal, written, visual, computational, embodied, artistic, or creative — assess the quality of the reasoning, not the mode used to show it.
Evidence you can grade

What You're Actually Looking For

Look for what students can explain, decide, or perform themselves. A polished answer or written reflection may need a follow-up question or a new example to check understanding.

Product aloneA polished essay. You have no idea if AI wrote half of it.
A useful checkThe same essay, plus the smallest useful trace — perhaps one revision note showing what changed, or a 90-second oral defense of one important decision.

For evidence of learning that lasts, revisit the skill later or ask students to apply it to a new task independently.
