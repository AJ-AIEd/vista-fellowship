For school leaders and professional-learning coordinators

A structured way for teachers to examine their own assessments

Policies about AI use do not tell a teacher whether a particular task still shows what a student understands. VISTA is a repeatable cycle in which teachers find that out for themselves, one assessment at a time, and leave a record of their reasoning.

What teachers actually do

Each teacher brings one assessment they already use. In the tool they audit it alone, test it with an AI tool the school already permits, decide what to change, teach it, and return to examine the evidence. The wider professional-learning journey includes supported exploration, research, dilemmas and peer conversation. The six assessment stages are described in the method.

You can try the whole experience now, with no account, including a completed example written to show what a cycle looks like.

What implementation requires

  • An estimated two to three hours per teacher per cycle, outside the teaching itself. This has not been measured.
  • Access to a generative AI tool under your own policies. VISTA does not provide one and works with any.
  • A modern browser. No installation, integration, rostering, or student accounts.
  • A person who convenes the group, if teachers are working together. The facilitator guide is written for that person.
  • An agreement that teachers’ working notes are private. See below.

Start with a supported pilot

The first step with any school is a small, paid pilot: one VISTA cycle with a group of teachers, supported by the author. A school licence is offered only afterwards, if the pilot shows the school wants to continue. The pilot is how both sides find out whether VISTA is useful in that school.

Proposed, not yet run. No school has completed a paid pilot. The structure below is a proposal; scope, support, and fee are agreed with each school in writing.

Pilot (proposed)

  • One cycle, from Initial Audit to Reflection, for a small group of teachers agreed with the school.
  • An onboarding session with the author for the coordinator and teachers.
  • Support from the author during the cycle, at points agreed in advance.
  • The self-guided tool, the printable protocol, and the facilitator guide in its current draft.
  • A closing conversation about what teachers chose to share and whether to continue.

Fee: set with each school. No pilot price has been published or tested.

Annual school licence (after a pilot)

Permission to run VISTA cycles as organised professional learning across one school for one academic year, with the materials below.

Price: the working range is USD 750–1,500 per school per year. This is a hypothesis. It has not been validated with any school and may change after the first pilots.

Not included

  • Extended workshops or coaching beyond what is agreed. These can be arranged separately.
  • AI tools or AI accounts.
  • Any report to school leaders about individual teachers. This does not exist and is not planned.
  • Rights to resell, sublicense, or publish the materials outside the school.

How access works today

There are optional individual accounts, but no school workspace and no administrator features. The tool is open on the web; a pilot or licence is an agreement about organised use, support, and materials. It is not enforced by technical access restrictions.

Individual teachers are welcome to use the tool for their own practice without a pilot or licence.

Before any agreement is signed. Written terms are still being prepared and need legal review. Nothing on this page is a contract.

Teacher privacy is a condition, not a feature

Teachers examine their own practice honestly only if their working notes are theirs. Guest notes stay in the teacher’s browser. Teachers may explicitly save copies to a personal account. There is no school dashboard or leader view; account storage is administered by the service operator under the privacy terms.

A teacher may choose to share a finished report with colleagues. That is the only route by which anything leaves their device. Schools that want aggregate information should ask teachers what they are willing to share. In a small group, even summary patterns can identify a person.

Details are on the privacy page.

Evidence: what exists and what does not

In useA facilitated pilot with a small group of teachers at one international school during 2026–2027.
GroundingThe design draws on published work on assessment validity, backward design, and teacher professional learning, listed in the tool’s references. Drawing on research is not the same as being validated by it.
Not yet knownWhether teachers’ assessment decisions improve; whether student learning is affected; whether the self-guided version works without a facilitator; whether results hold across subjects and schools.
What a pilot would addWith teachers’ consent, structured feedback on usability and on what changed in their tasks. We would share what we learn with you, including what did not work.

There are no testimonials or impact figures on this site because none have been earned yet.
